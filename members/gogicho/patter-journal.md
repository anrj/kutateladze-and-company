One section per pattern from your affinity map. For each, ask why, then ask why of the answer. Stop when the next answer would be a guess instead of something a quote supports. Three whys is often enough; five is a ceiling, not a target.

## Pattern 1: [When workers lack accessible infrastructure like a cafeteria, they view delivery fees as an unjustifiable luxury and will actively choose physical discomfort like starving over financial guilt]

**Interviews behind it:** log-08, log-10

1. Why does this happen? (answer in one sentence; name the log that supports it)
Because paying a high delivery fee for just one cheap meal makes them feel like they are "throwing money in the wind." (log-004)
2. Why that? Because coordinating a group order, collecting cash, and figuring out exact change with coworkers is too much of a social and logistical hassle. (guess)

**Root cause, one sentence:**

Coworkers choose to starve or feel financially guilty because the friction of organizing a group delivery order is currently higher than the discomfort of skipping lunch.

Answer 2 is a guess. No one explicitly said why they don't group order. I am assuming it's because coordinating money and orders is a hassle. In my next interview, I need to ask: "Tell me about the last time you tried to order food together with your coworkers."

## Pattern 2: [Systemic delays and timezone differences drain people's energy, meaning the actual cost they pay is arriving at their primary task already exhausted and biologically compromised]

**Interviews behind it:** log-07, log-09

1. Why does this happen? Because unpredictable transit delays and late-night shifts ruin their normal sleep and stress levels before their work even begins. (log-001, log-003)
2. Why that? Because their schedules are dictated entirely by external forces (overseas employers or unreliable transit drivers) that don't accommodate the user's reality. (log-001, log-003)

**Root cause, one sentence:** 

People sacrifice their baseline physical wellbeing because they are trapped in inflexible systems, transit or timezones, that prioritize the destination's schedule over the journey's toll.


## Pattern 3: [People facing strict environmental constraints (no nearby food, opposite timezones) resort to highly unhealthy biological workarounds just to survive the workday]

**Interviews behind it:** log-07, log-08, log-10

1. Why does this happen? Because they experience sudden sugar crashes or extreme fatigue and need immediate energy to keep functioning. (log-003, log-006)
2. Why that? Because they fail to prepare and bring healthy alternatives from home ahead of time. (guess)

**Root cause, one sentence:**

A lack of accessible healthy infrastructure at the exact moment of fatigue forces workers to rely on unhealthy stimulants to maintain their mandated productivity.

**Strongest pattern (most interviews, clearest root cause):**
Pattern 1 (The choice between starving and paying high delivery fees for workplace lunch).

**Why it is the strongest, in two sentences, citing log numbers:**
It reveals a highly specific, recurring financial pain point where users already know exactly what it costs them (40 GEL in log-08) and what biological toll it takes when they refuse to pay (log-10). Unlike fixing global timezones or intercity traffic, we actually have the power to solve this root cause by removing the friction of group-ordering to eliminate single-person delivery fees.
