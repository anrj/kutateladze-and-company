# Pattern journal · Davit Kutateladze (@QutaInParis)

Same prompt every week: Where did you apply this pattern this week, where did you exercise agency beyond your assigned lane, and what did you hack together to test something faster than the plan allowed? Link the DECISIONS.md lines you drove.

## Week 1 · Act like an owner, not a role
My accountability in the team contract is Product & Customer Discovery. I wrote my three problem statements directly from acute friction I watched firsthand: my family losing 220 GEL on a boiler repair because paper receipts vanished into home clutter, thermal receipts turning blank white within months, and small businesses getting audited over missing paper slips. Outside my discovery lane, when the team was blocked on repo setup, I created the initial directory tree, wrote the markdown templates for interview logs, and resolved git merge conflicts rather than waiting for our build lead. `00-foundation/problem-pool.md`, `00-foundation/team-contract.md`

## Week 2 · Evidence over opinion
When the team debated whether to build a broad consumer file storage app or focus strictly on physical receipts and bills, I pushed to score the Four Filters on verifiable losses instead of team opinions. Outside my lane, I drafted the Mom Test interview script v1, cold-contacted a practicing accountant, and conducted both Interview 01 and Interview 02 myself so we had empirical numbers: a 220 GEL out-of-pocket repair and a 500 GEL Revenue Service tax penalty. Hack: during the accountant interview, I asked her to screen-share her actual Google Drive on the spot to inspect how files were actually named (mostly unindexed "IMG_xxxx" files), immediately disproving the assumption that people manually organize digital folders. `00-foundation/four-filters-scorecard.md`, `01-discovery/interview-logs/03-house-owner.md`, `01-discovery/interview-logs/04-relative-accountant.md`, `DECISIONS.md` line: `Prioritize mobile receipt photo capture over desktop web dashboard`

## Week 3 · Kill your favorite idea
I entered the week convinced that our solution should be an elaborate desktop web dashboard with custom tagging, nested folders, and manual receipt entry. The interview evidence completely killed this idea: the accountant showed that employees never tag or rename files in Google Drive, and the homeowner confirmed nobody sits at a computer to manage paper. I discarded my own dashboard concept and rewrote the product requirements to center entirely around mobile camera snapping and automated OCR extraction. Outside my lane, I updated the technical architecture proposal and deleted 12 backlog user stories related to manual tag hierarchies. Hack: ran an informal 10-minute test asking two peers to manually log 3 receipts into a Google Form; both abandoned it halfway through, confirming that manual entry kills user adoption. `01-discovery/problem-hypothesis.md`, `DECISIONS.md` line: `Require automated receipt parsing (OCR) in MVP scope rather than manual entry`

## Week 4 · The prototype is the conversation
Instead of waiting for a fully wired frontend and database deployment, I used a prototype as the direct conversation starter. Crossing into the build lane, I hacked together a minimal Telegram bot hooked to a basic OCR script in an afternoon so we had a tangible artifact. I put the bot directly in front of the accountant and had her snap photos of three crumpled cash receipts from her desk; watching the bot misread a faded total prompted an immediate, concrete conversation about error handling and receipt verification that an abstract wireframe never could have triggered. Hack: manually corrected the backend parse errors in real time during the second test call (Wizard of Oz style) to test whether she valued the extracted summary even before our parsing pipeline was fully trained. `02-prototype/bot-prototype.py`, `02-prototype/feedback-log.md`, `DECISIONS.md` line: `Adopt Telegram bot interface for Week 4 prototype testing`
