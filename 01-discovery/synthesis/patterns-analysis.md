# Patterns Analysis
One section per pattern from your affinity map. For each, ask why, then ask why of the answer. Stop when the next answer would be a guess instead of something a quote supports. Three whys is often enough; five is a ceiling, not a target.

## Pattern 1: A missing paper document costs people real money, even when the payment or the right behind it was never in doubt

**Interviews behind it:** 03, 04

1. Why does this happen? Because the paper itself is the proof, not the payment or the right. The homeowner was entitled to a free replacement but only had to pay because he could not show the warranty (03); the company had the bank record of the payment and was still fined, because the physical slip could not be produced (04).
2. Why can they not produce it? Because it is somewhere they cannot find. The homeowner "knew I had the document somewhere" (03); the accountant's "physical slip simply vanished" (04).
3. Why can they not find it when it matters? Because it is needed in rare, urgent moments (a boiler failing in winter, a tax audit), long after it was filed. The log records a search "once or twice a year" against paper arriving "almost every week" (03), and a monthly deadline crunch (04).

**Root cause, one sentence:** Documents arrive constantly and are filed without any thought of the rare, urgent moment when one of them becomes the only proof of something worth hundreds of lari.

**Which of the answers above is a guess rather than a quote? (be honest; this becomes a question for your next interview):** Answer 3 is partly inferred. The frequency lines come from the logs, but nobody said "we don't file carefully because it only matters rarely". Ask the next interviewee what they were thinking when they filed the paper that later went missing.

## Pattern 2: Documents end up in many places, filed by different people in different ways, so nobody can find or confirm one when it is needed

**Interviews behind it:** 03, 04

1. Why does this happen? Each person files in their own place. The homeowner keeps receipts in the hallway drawer while his wife uses the kitchen shelf (03); staff send the accountant photos named "IMG_2041" (04).
2. Why does the filing differ? Nobody agreed on one place or one naming, and each person files where it is convenient for them at that moment. (03, 04. The logs show the effect; no one stated a reason.)
3. Why does a system that exists still fail? The accountant did build one, with folders by month on Google Drive, and it is still "chaos" because other people feed it (04). A shared place does not help if the contents arrive unnamed, blurry or missing (04).

**Root cause, one sentence:** The documents are created and collected by many people at many moments, so any single place someone sets up is only as good as the least careful person who feeds it.

**Which of the answers above is a guess rather than a quote?** Answer 2 is a guess: both logs show different people filing differently, but nobody said why. Ask what makes it easy or hard to file a paper the moment it arrives.

## Pattern 3: A record kept by hand in one person's notebook, chat or folder cannot settle a dispute, so the person who kept it absorbs the loss

**Interviews behind it:** 01, 04, 06

1. Why does this happen? The record belongs to the keeper alone, so the other side can simply say it is wrong. The shop seller shows her notebook and customers "still tell me I'm mistaken" (01); the tutor "couldn't prove it, so I let it go" (06).
2. Why does the keeper give in? Because proving it is harder than losing it, or the relationship matters more. The tutor lets it go (06) and the seller asks "what am I supposed to do?" (01). In 04, by contrast, the company had no choice because the tax authority required the original paper.
3. Why is the record weak in the first place? It is written when the keeper remembers to, in a place only the keeper controls. The tutor says "sometimes I forget to write it down" (06).

**Root cause, one sentence:** A hand-kept record is only the keeper's word, so when the other party disagrees, the keeper has nothing that both sides accept.

**Which of the answers above is a guess rather than a quote?** The "relationship matters more" part of answer 2 is inferred from the tutor's and seller's tone, not stated. Ask what would have to exist for them to hold their ground.

**Strongest pattern (most interviews, clearest root cause):** Pattern 1, a missing paper document costs real money.

**Why it is the strongest, in two sentences, citing log numbers:** Both documents interviews (03, 04) raised it on their own, and each gave a number: 220 GEL paid for a repair still under warranty (03) and a 500 GEL fine (04). Its root cause also points at something specific, the moment of need, which is what the problem statement is built around.

**An honest limit:** only logs 03 and 04 are about documents directly. Patterns 1 and 2 rest on two interviews each, which is the rubric minimum, and both interviewees are adults managing a household or a small company.
