## Cluster 1: Missing Proof Causes Direct Financial Loss

**Description:**
(a sentence that says what the evidence shows, not a one-word topic):** A missing paper document costs people real money, even when the payment or the right behind it was never in doubt.

**Number of notes:** 10

**Quotes (verbatim, each tagged with its log number):**
- "When the central heating boiler shut down in the middle of winter, I knew I had the document somewhere, but we couldn't find it anywhere. The kids were freezing, so we just called another repairman and paid 220 GEL right away." (03)
- "When the repairman changed the part, he told me this looks like a factory defect and you were entitled to a free replacement. So I lost out twice just because of a lost piece of paper." (03)
- "Last year the Revenue Service audited us and because we couldn't produce the original primary document for one expense, they fined us 500 GEL. We knew it was paid, but the physical slip simply vanished." (04)
- "Last year our warranty on a 180 GEL printer was rejected simply because the date on the receipt was no longer readable." (11)
- "Twice we paid the exact same supplier invoice because I thought the old paper had been filed and the payment didn't show up in my notes." (12)
- "Last month we couldn't find the fridge service receipt during an inspection and got a formal warning, even though we had paid for and completed the service two weeks earlier." (15)

**Interviews represented:** 03, 04, 11, 12, 15

## Cluster 2: Fragmented Storage Across People, Locations, and Channels

**Description:**
Documents end up in many places, filed by different people in different ways, so nobody can find or confirm one when it is needed.

**Number of notes:** 10

**Quotes:**
- "Everyone puts receipts in different places — I dump them in the hallway drawer, my wife puts them on a kitchen shelf. In the end, no one even knows if we still have that paper or threw it away." (03)
- "I made folders on Google Drive broken down by month, but it's still chaos — people name files 'IMG_2041' and then I spend hours searching which receipt belongs to which invoice." (04)
- "When I have to submit tax declarations at the end of the month, I have to beg employees one by one on WhatsApp to send photos of expense receipts. Half are lost, half are blurry photos where numbers aren't visible." (04)
- "Couriers bring paper receipts, some come by email, some on Messenger or WhatsApp - nothing collects in one place, and in the end we pay twice or get late fees." (11)
- "I send my kid's clinic lab results and payment receipts to my husband on WhatsApp 'so we have them saved,' then that chat fills up with photos and we can't find anything by searching." (13)
- "We tell students to email their payment receipts, but some write to my personal email, some drop them in the dean's office chat, and some bring printed slips in person, reconciling it with the registry takes a whole week." (15)

**Interviews represented:** 03, 04, 11, 13, 14

## Process Reflections

### What Surprised Us
We initially thought people lost money because they lacked a folder or storage spot. Instead, almost every interviewee already used folders, ring binders, Google Drive, or WhatsApp chats, yet still lost 140–500+ GEL because scattered channels, generic filenames, and faded thermal ink made retrieval fail under deadline or emergency pressure.

### Challenges We Faced
Our first 10 exploratory interviews covered multiple candidate problem areas (shop credit, tutoring, lunch, commuting, apartment hunting, and documents), so only 2 logs (03, 04) focused strictly on physical/digital document keeping while 2 others (01, 06) overlapped on manual record disputes. Separating domain-specific quotes from unrelated exploratory logs was a necessary challenge to avoid forcing weak evidence. We had to hold and log 5 new interviews.

### Key Learnings
Capture 
is not the bottleneck, retrieval with proof is. The pain spikes in high-stakes moments and is compounded by unindexed files, multi-person fragmentation, and faded paper.
