## Cluster 1: Missing Proof Causes Direct Financial Loss

**Pattern (a sentence that says what the evidence shows, not a one-word topic):** A missing paper document costs people real money, even when the payment or the right behind it was never in doubt.

**Interviews behind it (2 minimum):** 03, 04

**Quotes (verbatim, each tagged with its log number):**
- "When the central heating boiler shut down in the middle of winter, I knew I had the document somewhere, but we couldn't find it anywhere. The kids were freezing, so we just called another repairman and paid 220 GEL right away." (03)
- "When the repairman changed the part, he told me this looks like a factory defect and you were entitled to a free replacement. So I lost out twice just because of a lost piece of paper." (03)
- "Last year the Revenue Service audited us and because we couldn't produce the original primary document for one expense, they fined us 500 GEL. We knew it was paid, but the physical slip simply vanished." (04)

## Cluster 2: Fragmented Storage Across People, Locations, and Channels

**Pattern:** Documents end up in many places, filed by different people in different ways, so nobody can find or confirm one when it is needed.

**Interviews behind it:** 03, 04

**Quotes:**
- "Everyone puts receipts in different places — I dump them in the hallway drawer, my wife puts them on a kitchen shelf. In the end, no one even knows if we still have that paper or threw it away." (03)
- "I made folders on Google Drive broken down by month, but it's still chaos — people name files 'IMG_2041' and then I spend hours searching which receipt belongs to which invoice." (04)
- "When I have to submit tax declarations at the end of the month, I have to beg employees one by one on WhatsApp to send photos of expense receipts. Half are lost, half are blurry photos where numbers aren't visible." (04)

## Process Reflections

### What Surprised Us
We initially assumed people lost money from missing documents because they had no place to store them. Instead, both document-focused interviewees (Log 03, Log 04) already had dedicated storage, a hallway plastic folder and a Google Drive + ring binder. Yet, they still lost 220 and 500 GEL because retrieval failed under pressure.

### Challenges We Faced
Our first 10 exploratory interviews covered multiple candidate problem areas (shop credit, tutoring, lunch, commuting, apartment hunting, and documents), so only 2 logs (03, 04) focused strictly on physical/digital document keeping while 2 others (01, 06) overlapped on manual record disputes. Separating domain-specific quotes from unrelated exploratory logs was a necessary challenge to avoid forcing weak evidence.

### Key Learnings
Keeping a paper or photo is easy, finding the right proof at the exact moment of an audit, warranty claim, or payment dispute is where current workflows break. To raise our confidence before building, we need additional targeted interviews specifically with household and small-office document keepers.
