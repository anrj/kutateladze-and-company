# Problem Statement

## The statement (one paragraph, five sentences, each sentence ends with log numbers)

1. **Who has the problem:** Adults who are the keepers of the paper a household or a small company depends on, such as warranty cards, receipts and expense slips, like a 48-year-old homeowner in a family of four and a 36-year-old accountant for two small companies in Tbilisi. (03, 04)
2. **When it happens:** On the day one specific paper suddenly matters, when the central heating boiler fails in the middle of winter, or when the end-of-month tax declaration is due and the receipts must be reconciled. (03, 04)
3. **What it costs them:** 220 GEL paid for a repair that was still under warranty plus half a day searching drawers (03), and a 500 GEL tax fine plus 6 to 8 hours every month chasing missing slips (04). (03, 04)
4. **What they do about it today:** A plastic folder in the hallway cabinet, with receipts also left in drawers, on a kitchen shelf and in pockets (03); a shared Google Drive with folders by month, a ring binder for paper originals, and begging employees on WhatsApp for photos of receipts (04). (03, 04)
5. **Why that fails them:** "I made folders on Google Drive broken down by month, but it's still chaos — people name files 'IMG_2041' and then I spend hours searching which receipt belongs to which invoice." (04); "In the end, no one even knows if we still have that paper or threw it away." (03)

## What the evidence ruled out

We began without a settled problem, and our shop-credit idea ("nisia") was dropped after one interview: the seller raised unpaid and disputed amounts only after we asked, and she has no computer (01); see line 3 of `DECISIONS.md`. For documents, the logs also ruled out one belief: that people lack a place to keep them. Both interviewees already had one, a hallway folder (03) and a Drive plus binder (04), and they still lost money.

## What is still open

Only two of our ten logs (03, 04) are about documents, and both interviewees are adults running a household or a small company. No log tells us whether people with fewer papers, such as students, have the same problem, or whether they would keep using a new way of filing.

## Pattern behind it

- Pattern: A missing paper document costs people real money, even when the payment or the right behind it was never in doubt. (03, 04)
- Root cause: Documents arrive constantly and are filed without any thought of the rare, urgent moment when one of them becomes the only proof of something worth hundreds of lari.
